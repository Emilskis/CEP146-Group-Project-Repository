## Historical cybersecurity incident
### Overview
In 2014, Yahoo had a serious data breach, and the information of more than 500 million users was stolen by hackers. What's worse, Yahoo didn't disclose this matter to the public until two years later. Later, U.S. investigators found that the attack was related to the Russian intelligence agency (FSB). Hackers used harpoon phishing emails and other methods to break into Yahoo's system and obtain sensitive information such as the user's name, email address, password with weak encryption, birthday, phone number, and answers to security questions. After the incident was exposed, Yahoo was forced to ask users to modify passwords, enable multiple authentication (MFA), and improve its security protection, such as improving encryption methods and intrusion detection systems. The leak, together with the incident that affected all 3 billion accounts in 2013, is considered the largest data breach in history. The consequences were very serious - Yahoo's user trust plummeted, and the company faced lawsuits and huge compensation. It was finally acquired by Verizon in 2017, and the acquisition price was reduced by $350 million. The incident also made people realize that if enterprises conceal data leakage, they will not only lose trust, but also bring huge legal and economic costs. It has also promoted the introduction of data protection regulations such as GDPR, requiring companies to take stricter responsibility for user information.
